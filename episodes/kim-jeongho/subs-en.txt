They say the woodblocks of Daedongyeojido (Great Map of the East Land) were burned and destroyed. But they are actually in a museum right now.
Joseon's mapmaker, Gosanja Kim Jeong-ho. He's a man whose birth, death, and social status aren't even recorded.
The Daedongyeojido he created was a 6.7-meter-tall, 22-fold map; a nationwide map carved into woodblocks to be printed and copied.
But the story we learned was different: he traveled the country 3 times, climbed Mt. Baekdu 8 times, and died in prison.
That's entirely the Kim Jeong-ho depicted by the Japanese Government-General's textbooks; it started with a 1925 newspaper article, and the Annals have no record of his imprisonment.
As for the woodblocks said to be lost, 11 of them remain as national treasures in the National Museum of Korea.
