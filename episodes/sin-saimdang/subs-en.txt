Joseon never once called this person on the 50,000-won bill a "hyeonmoyangcheo" (good wife and wise mother).
During King Myeongjong's reign, Eo Suk-gwon evaluated her: "Her grapes and landscapes are so exquisite, she is second only to An Gyeon."
Yes, the very An Gyeon who painted the "Mongyudowondo" (Dream Journey to the Peach Blossom Land).
Back then, men married into their wives' families, and wealth was divided equally among sons and daughters; Saimdang also lived with her parents in Gangneung until she was 38.
Actually, "hyeonmoyangcheo" was a phrase that didn't exist in Joseon; it was a modern educational slogan imported from Japan.
It first appeared in 1906, exactly 355 years after her death.
Even now, the front of the 50,000-won bill features the very grapes that Eo Suk-gwon called exquisite.
