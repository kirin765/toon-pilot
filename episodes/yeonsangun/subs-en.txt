The phrase "heungcheong-mangcheong" (to wildly party away) actually came from a single king.
Yeonsangun, the 10th king of Joseon, belatedly learned that his mother had died by poison.
In revenge, he purged hundreds of officials and even abolished the offices that offered criticism.
He drafted 10,000 gisaeng (courtesans) from across the country to turn the palace into a playground, calling them "Heungcheong."
The people said the country would be ruined (mangcheong) because of the Heungcheong, and eventually, the officials dragged the king down.
His name was erased and he simply became Yeonsangun, but the phrase he created still remains today: Heungcheong-mangcheong.
