You'd think the word "nanjangpan" (total chaos) came from a market? No, it's from the Joseon gwageo (state exam) site.
Over 100,000 people flocked to the civil service exam that only selected 33.
Since the examiners only graded the first submitted papers, a good seat meant passing; people hired thugs to reserve seats, and some were even crushed to death.
According to Jeong Yak-yong's records, a "geobyeok" (ghostwriter) wrote the text, a "sasu" (scribe) did the writing, and the actual candidate just sat there.
Looking at this exam site drawn by Kim Hong-do, his teacher Kang Se-hwang called it a fight of 10,000 ants.
This pandemonium was called "nanjang," which became today's "nanjangpan."
