# 자율 루프 - 4. 기존 편 다국어 확장 (영어) (r9-12)

[EP.1 장영실]
Joseon's greatest scientist was actually a slave.
Born as a government slave in Dongnae, Jang Yeong-sil's incredible craftsmanship reached the king's ears.
King Sejong erased his slave status and brought him to the palace.
In 1434, he invented the Jagyeokru, a water clock that automatically struck a bell to tell time.
A man who was once a slave rose to the rank of Daehogun (3rd senior rank).
But when the king's palanquin he built broke, he was beaten 80 times, dismissed, and vanished without a trace.

[EP.2 김시습]
A genius acknowledged by King Sejong refused public office for his entire life.
He could write poetry at age 5, and his name was literally Ose (Five Years Old); even King Sejong marveled at him.
But at 21, when King Sejo usurped the throne from his nephew, he wept bitterly for three days.
Kim Si-seup burned all his books, shaved his head, and became a monk.
The story he wrote in Namsan, Gyeongju, after long wandering, is Korea's first novel written in Chinese characters.
Until he closed his eyes at 59 in Muryangsa Temple, he never accepted a government post.

[EP.3 정약용]
The king's most beloved genius became a criminal overnight.
Jeong Yak-yong, who enjoyed King Jeongjo's absolute favor, was a talent who invented the Geojunggi crane and helped build Hwaseong Fortress.
But as soon as Jeongjo died, he was swept up in the Catholic persecution and exiled to far-off Gangjin.
That exile lasted a staggering 18 years.
However, instead of despairing, he picked up his brush.
The books he wrote at Dasanchodang during those 18 years number over 500, including Mokminsimseo.
At the place where his life seemed to end, Joseon's greatest scholarship was born.

[EP.4 광해군]
The king gave a strange order to a general heading off to war.
He told him not to fight blindly, but to read the situation before acting.
This king was Gwanghaegun, born of a concubine and rejected five times for the crown prince title.
He executed a tightrope extreme diplomacy between the Ming Dynasty and Later Jin, provoking neither.
But he left behind the stigma of confining the queen dowager and killing his brother.
Ultimately, he was overthrown in a coup and spent the rest of his life exiled on Jeju Island.
Was he an incompetent tyrant or a strategist ahead of his time?

[EP.5 연산군]
The expression 'Heungcheong-mangcheong' (squandering money) actually comes from a king.
Yeonsangun, the 10th king of Joseon, belatedly found out his mother was poisoned to death.
For revenge, he executed hundreds of officials and even abolished the offices that gave him bad advice.
He drafted 10,000 gisaeng (courtesans) nationwide to turn the palace into a playground and called them Heungcheong.
The people said the country would be ruined because of the Heungcheong, and the officials eventually overthrew him.
Even his title of King was erased, leaving only Yeonsangun, but the phrase he created, 'Heungcheong-mangcheong', lives on today.
Thirty seconds of history. Subscribe for the next episode.

[EP.6 난장판]
Do you think the word 'Nanjangpan' (absolute chaos) comes from the marketplace? No, it comes from the Joseon state exams.
For the civil service exam that only selected 33 people, over 100,000 candidates swarmed in.
Since examiners graded the first submitted papers first, getting a good seat meant passing, so they hired thugs to hold spots and people were even crushed to death.
According to Jeong Yak-yong's records, a ghostwriter wrote the essay, a calligrapher copied it, and the candidate just sat there.
Seeing this exam site painted by Kim Hong-do, his teacher Kang Se-hwang called it 'a battle of ten thousand ants.'
This extreme chaos was called 'Nanjang', which is where today's 'Nanjangpan' comes from.
Thirty seconds of history. Subscribe for the next episode.

[EP.7 신사임당]
Joseon never called the person on this 50,000 won bill a 'good wife and wise mother'.
The evaluation by Eo Suk-gwon during King Myeongjong's reign was: Her grapes and landscapes are exquisite, second only to An Gyeon.
The very same An Gyeon who painted the Dream Journey to the Peach Blossom Land.
Back then, men moved into the wife's family home, and property was divided equally among sons and daughters. Saimdang also lived at her mother's house in Gangneung until she was 38.
In fact, the phrase 'good wife and wise mother' didn't exist in Joseon; it was a modern educational slogan imported from Japan.
It first appeared in 1906, 355 years after her death.
Even today, on the front of the 50,000 won bill, the grapes that Eo Suk-gwon praised so highly are printed.
Thirty seconds of history. Subscribe for the next episode.

[EP.8 명량]
Myeongnyang, where they defeated 133 ships with only 13. But for a moment, it was actually just one ship.
It was right after the entire Joseon navy had been wiped out in the previous battle.
The narrow Uldolmok strait. From the Nanjung Ilgi (War Diary): "Looking at the ships of the generals, I saw they had retreated to the open sea and were just watching."
While 12 ships stood by, a single flagship fought alone, firing its cannons relentlessly.
Yi Sun-sin raised a flag to call the generals, asking if they wanted to die under martial law.
Only then did the ships rush in, and together with the violent water currents, 31 enemy ships were smashed.
Yi Sun-sin recorded the victory with just a few words: "This was truly the help of heaven."
Then he moved the camp saying the tide had ebbed, and that was the end of the diary entry.
Thirty seconds of history. Subscribe for the next episode.

[EP.9 김정호]
You heard the wooden blocks of the Daedongyeojido map were burned and lost, right? But they are still in the museum today.
Joseon's mapmaker, Gosanja Kim Jeong-ho. He is someone whose birth year, death, and status aren't even recorded in history.
The Daedongyeojido he made was a 22-booklet map standing 6.7 meters tall, a reproducible national map carved on woodblocks.
However, the history we learned was different: that he traveled the country three times, climbed Mt. Baekdu eight times, and died in prison.
All of that Kim Jeong-ho was fabricated by Japanese colonial textbooks. It started with a newspaper article in 1925, and there is no record of his imprisonment in the Royal Annals.
Of those woodblocks that supposedly vanished, 11 of them remain in the National Museum of Korea as a treasure.
Thirty seconds of history. Subscribe for the next episode.

[EP.10 한글 창제]
Joseon's greatest invention suddenly appears in the Annals in a single sentence, without any warning.
"This month, His Majesty personally created 28 letters of Eonmun (the Korean alphabet)." There is no record of any preparation process before that.
Do you think the Jiphyeonjeon scholars did it for him? What the deputy director of Jiphyeonjeon, Choe Man-ri, submitted two months later was a letter of opposition.
He argued that if they made letters different from China, they would become barbarians.
Sejong retorted: "Do you even know the rhyming dictionaries? Exactly how many consonant and vowel letters are there?"
And thus Hangeul became the only alphabet in the world that retains a book explaining who made it, when, and with what principles.
Thirty seconds of history. Subscribe for the next episode.

[EP.11 간송 전형필]
Someone offered to sell a national treasure for just 1,000 won, and another person paid ten times the asking price.
In 1940 in Andong, the book explaining the principles behind the creation of Hangeul came to light.
In an era when the Japanese Empire oppressed our language and writing, the danger of even this book being taken away was immense.
The wealthiest man in Joseon, Jeon Hyeong-pil, handed over 10,000 won, saying that one must pay the price of a treasure for a treasure.
When the Korean War broke out, he put this book in a paulownia wood box and protected it by using it as a pillow.
The book that almost vanished for 1,000 won has now become a priceless national treasure.
Thirty seconds of history. Subscribe for the next episode.
